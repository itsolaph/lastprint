Reading the market clock

From the closing bell to the opening bell.

An order lives for exactly one closed-market window. The times below are New York time.

  1. 4:00 pm

    New York time

    The bell rings. We record the official close for every listed stock.

  2. Overnight

    Nights, weekends and US holidays

    You set a price relative to that close. A keeper fills your order on Base the moment the pool reaches it.

  3. 9:30 am

    New York time

    Not filled by the opening bell? The order dies on its own. Nothing to cancel.

What's under the hood.

Lastprint is a thin layer over public pieces on Base. Here is the path one order takes, and what each piece is there for.

  1. 1

    Your wallet

    Approves exactly the order amount and signs the order offchain (EIP-712). Your money stays in your wallet.

  2. 2

    Lastprint keeper

    Holds the last official close, follows the NYSE calendar and asks the pool for a price every 5 seconds.

  3. 3

    ActionFeed

    The brake. If a split or dividend adjustment is about to land, the fill is refused.

  4. 4

    Lastprint contract

    Checks your signature, the time window and your minimum, then pulls the funds and swaps in one transaction.

  5. 5

    Aerodrome pool

    Where the trade happens. The stock token lands straight in your wallet, or the whole thing reverts.

Fair questions

Why only when the market is closed?

Because that is when the close means something. While New York trades, the stock has a live exchange price and yesterday's close is stale. Between the closing bell and the next opening bell, the last official close is the one fixed reference, and the tokens keep trading on Base. An order is valid only inside that window, and the contract checks the window on every fill.

What is the last close, and where does it come from?

It is the official closing price of the regular US session for each stock. Lastprint reads it from the Yahoo Finance chart API, with the Nasdaq.com historical API as the fallback, and shows the source and session date next to every price. Both are free, unofficial endpoints, so the close is shown for reference. What binds is the minimum amount written into the order you sign.

What happens on a split or dividend?

Tokenized stocks carry a multiplier that changes on splits and dividend adjustments. Every order is bound to the multiplier you saw when you signed it. ActionFeed pauses fills when a corporate action is pending or the issuer has paused transfers, so an order signed against one multiplier never fills against another.

Who holds my money?

Nobody. Your tokens stay in your wallet until the fill. At fill time the contract pulls exactly the amount you approved, swaps it through an allowlisted router and sends the result straight to you. The contract has no owner, no admin and no stored funds.

What does it cost?

Lastprint charges no fee. You pay gas once, for the approval. Signing an order is free, and the keeper pays the gas for the fill. Cancelling in the app is free too; the optional onchain cancel is a transaction and costs gas.

Can I use Lastprint from the United States?

No. Lastprint is not available to US persons or in the US and its territories. Orders from US locations are refused, and the order ticket stays read-only there. You can still browse closes and Base prices.
